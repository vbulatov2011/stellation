Stellations under different rules

"The fifty-nine icosahedra" is not a count of what the icosahedron has. It is a count of what survives a particular set of rules — Miller's — about what a stellation is allowed to be. Change one rule and the number changes with it.

These seven lists are Guy Inchbald's, each computed by applying a different set of acceptance conditions to the same arrangement of twenty planes. The canonical 59 is the first. The others tighten it, by demanding that a face plane not be broken or an edge not be segmented, or loosen it, by dropping Miller's rule (v) — the ban on shells separated from the core — and putting a continuity rule in its place.

Between them they name 84 distinct figures, 25 of which the canonical list rejects. Every one is a document: click it and the app opens with those cells chosen.

59 The 59 icosahedra

Miller's rules as Coxeter, Du Val, Flather and Petrie applied them — the canonical list.

  • No enantiomorphs
  • No hidden cavities
  • No separated shells
 34 The 59, with continuous face planes

The canonical rules with one more: a face plane may not be broken. It removes 25 of the 59.

  • No enantiomorphs
  • No hidden cavities
  • No separated shells
  • Face planes continuous
 36 Continuous faces instead of rule (v)

Drop the ban on separated shells and require continuous face planes in its place. Two figures the canonical list rejects come back.

  • No enantiomorphs
  • No hidden cavities
  • Face planes continuous

2 of them are not among the 59.

 25 The 59, with continuous edges

The canonical rules plus a ban on segmented edges — the harshest cut of the canonical list, leaving 25.

  • No enantiomorphs
  • No hidden cavities
  • No separated shells
  • No segmented edges
 50 Continuous edges instead of rule (v)

Drop the ban on separated shells and require unsegmented edges instead. This is the one set that admits figures far outside the 59 — shells floating free of the core.

  • No enantiomorphs
  • No hidden cavities
  • No segmented edges

25 of them are not among the 59.

 25 The 59, with continuous faces and edges

Both continuity rules on top of the canonical ones. It happens to select the same 25 as the edge rule alone.

  • No enantiomorphs
  • No hidden cavities
  • No separated shells
  • No segmented edges
  • Face planes continuous
 27 Continuous faces and edges instead of rule (v)

Both continuity rules, with the ban on separated shells dropped. Two more than the subset above.

  • No enantiomorphs
  • No hidden cavities
  • No segmented edges
  • Face planes continuous

2 of them are not among the 59.

The lists come from Stellation Lists under Different Rules by Guy Inchbald, read on 2026-08-16. They were computed by a program of his, printed on a dot-matrix printer and later scanned, and each entry carries a numeric cell code as well as a printed symbol. The figures here are built from the codes; every symbol was regenerated from its code and checked against the printed one, which found one casualty of the scanning (entry 735 of the first list) and no disagreement anywhere else.
