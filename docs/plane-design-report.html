Technical report · Stellation engine

Plane representations and the reachable polyhedra

What is missing before more of Brückner's 146 models become clickable, and which engine design choices govern what the stellation app can show at all.
Prepared 6 August 2026 · from V. Bulatov's feedback session, the ported source, and a 150-solid build census · repo stellation-resurrect · the plates, clickable · Brückner 1900 · the app

1Summary

Vladimir described what he called the program's fundamental defect: planes are carried through the pipeline as a single 3-D point — the foot of the perpendicular from the origin — so a plane through the origin cannot be represented at all. That account is accurate for the Java applet, and §3 traces it to the exact line. But in the JavaScript port the story has already half-changed: planes are stored as unit normal + offset {n, d} — four numbers — and the surviving problem is not the representation but three assumptions layered on it: every plane is flipped to d > 0, planes with d ≈ 0 are silently discarded, and the layer/cell semantics presume a core cell that contains the origin.

Measured against the actual goal — how many of Brückner's 146 photographed models can be rebuilt live — the diametral-plane fix turns out to be the smallest of five levers. The census of all 146 figures (§4) gives this ladder:

Also found while measuring

Seven duals that are in today's catalog menu — d23 d26 d44 d55 d61 d68 d78 — currently build silently wrong arrangements. The cause is not diametral planes but a second, previously undiagnosed bug: their faces are antiparallelograms (bow-ties), whose two lobes have equal areas, so the Newell normal the port computes cancels to ≈0 and the face planes come out as noise (§3.4). This is fixable independently of everything else in this report, and should come first.

2Where things stand

The clickable-plates page currently rebuilds 16 of 146 figures, every one verified in the engine (vertex/face/volume/outer-radius). The data layer is far larger than the app reveals: geometry.json ships 150 solids — the 75 Kaleido uniforms u06–u80 (file uNN = Maeder U(NN−5)) and their 75 duals — of which the catalog menus expose 121. The 29 orphans are the nine hemipolyhedra, their nine duals, nine duplicate self-dual twins, and u80/d80 (the great dirhombicosidodecahedron and dual). A build census over all 150 files: every file builds under its best symmetry, in 0.03–7 s; the heaviest (d65 d45 d79…) reach ≈23,000 primitive cells at the default depth cap.

Two backdoors matter for what follows. The URL hash loader accepts any geometry key — #u08 loads the octahemioctahedron today, but with a synthesized Ih symmetry that is wrong for an octahedral solid. And parseStel already reads the applet's planes field from .stel files; the preset layer simply discards it. Both are half-built doors into the capabilities this report costs out.

3The plane representation, from Java to the port

Planes are stored as a point in space — the point of the plane closest to the origin — so a plane passing exactly through the origin cannot be expressed at all. That choice, made at the very beginning, is why a significant number of the polyhedra cannot be reproduced, and probably why many of Brückner’s figures cannot be built either.

“My planes are specified by a point in space… so I cannot specify a plane through the origin. It was a fundamental mistake at the very start of development… a significant share of polyhedra cannot be reproduced… possibly many of Brückner's too.”

— V. Bulatov, feedback session, 6 Aug 2026. His proposed fix: normal (three numbers, not necessarily unit) + distance to the origin — four numbers.

3.1 Where the point encoding lives in the Java applet

The Java Plane class itself already stores normal + signed distance (v·X − d = 0). The defect is one step downstream: StellationController.makePolyhedronPlanes re-encodes every plane as planes[i] = plane.v.mul(plane.d) — the foot of the perpendicular — and Stellation.makeStellationFaces reconstructs the plane as v = normalize(point), d = |point|. At d = 0 the point is the zero vector and the normal is gone; and since d = |point| ≥ 0, every normal is forced to point away from the origin. Exactly as The original author recalled — one lossy round-trip in the middle of an otherwise four-number pipeline.

3.2 What the port actually does

// docs/lib/core.js — facePlanes()
if (d < 0) { n = mul(n, -1); d = -d; }   // orient away from the origin
if (Math.abs(d) < 1e-9) continue;        // plane through the centre: skip

The representation is Vladimir's proposed four numbers already; the two lines above are the inherited policy. Note the port converts the Java behaviour from loud to silent: the applet's plane-from-point constructor degenerates visibly, while the port just drops the plane — no console message, no count. The vestigial planeFromPoint() has no remaining callers and substitutes an arbitrary normal (0,0,1) at |p| < 1e-12.

Loading any of these through the hash backdoor today silently produces the arrangement of the surviving planes only — u09 quietly becomes a tetrahedron study. On the Brückner side, the census (§4) shows the fix is a prerequisite for four figures: VIII-1 (the octahemioctahedron — and, being the one orientable hemipolyhedron, it is honestly rebuildable as cells once its diametral hexagons exist), XII-1 (small dodecahemicosahedron), and Brückner's own degenerate-parameter varieties IX-1 and IX-18, where he moves a family of dodecahedron planes until they pass through the centre. Vladimir's hunch that “possibly many of Brückner's” fail for this reason is right in kind — the book deliberately visits the degenerate case — but small in count: four figures of 146.

What the real fix requires beyond un-skipping: (a) an explicit orientation convention per diametral plane (both half-spaces touch the centre, so “outward” is a choice, not a fact); (b) generalized layer semantics — today layer 0 is the cell containing the origin, and with a diametral plane no cell contains it (for u09 the centre sits on three planes at once and the “core” is eight octants); (c) UI: the Cells table, the {0} presets, and the walkthrough all assume a core row. None of this touches the clipping algebra itself — the Java constructions show the arrangement code nowhere needs d > 0.

3.4 The bug the measurement found instead: bow-tie faces

The census flagged five more “diametral” files — and adversarial verification refuted the diagnosis in the most useful way. d44 d55 d61 d68 (small rhombidodecacron, small dodecicosacron, rhombicosacron, great dodecicosacron) have no diametral faces: they are isohedral, every face at one nonzero distance. But their faces are antiparallelograms — crossed quadrilaterals whose two lobes have equal area with opposite winding — so the Newell area-vector the port uses as the face normal cancels to ≈0, and facePlanes emits garbage normals with scattered distances (spread 80:1 where the true solid has 1:1). One face per file lands at exactly 0 and trips the diametral filter; the other 59 pass through as noise. The same degeneracy exists, undetected by any filter, in d23 d26 d78 — and all seven of these are in the catalog today. (d70, the ninth flag, is the genuine hemi-dual with Maeder's finite fudge for its ideal vertices.)

Immediate, independent fix

When |Newell normal| ≈ 0, fit the plane from the (coplanar) vertices instead — any non-collinear triple. One guarded branch in facePlanes repairs seven currently-wrong catalog solids and needs no new semantics at all.

4The unlock ladder: what each design choice buys on the plates

Every one of the 146 figures was classified by what would make it clickable, using the book's own per-figure descriptions (145 of 146 are cited in the text), the shipped data files, and computed face inventories. The full table is in the appendix; here is each rung with its mechanism and its evidence.

4.1 D1 — no engine change: 16 → 55

Three groups need only derivation work against arrangements the engine already builds:

Self-stars (17 figures). Brückner photographed seventeen orientable uniform star polyhedra that are in the catalog right now; each equals the solid union of cells in its own face-plane arrangement. This is provable mechanically: compute the winding number of the star surface around a representative point of each sub-cell orbit. Run tonight as a demonstration, with orbit-consistency checks:

The probe failure is the honest caveat: u55 is one of the non-orientable non-hemi uniforms, where winding is ill-defined — the tool must refuse non-orientable inputs and double-check orbit unanimity. The seventeen self-star figures are all orientable.

Derivation-only (9 figures). The §157–158 solids — Brückner's 15th-kind and 45th-kind isogonal-isohedral solids and their polars, plus XI-11 on the deltoidal icositetrahedron — lie in the planes of catalog solids (d29, d15). Same situation the compound of five cubes was in yesterday before it fell to a membership computation ({0,1,2,3,4} on d29); these need Brückner's ring-point constructions read out of pp. 209–212 and turned into selections.

Dual-stars with existing kernels (13 figures). Much of Plate X and XI is the polar (isohedral) mate of a uniform star, and thirteen of those mates are literally files in the catalog — e.g. X-28 is the medial rhombic triacontahedron (d41), X-30 the small stellapentakisdodecahedron (d42), XI-17 the great rhombic triacontahedron (d59). “Rebuild” here means: load the dual file, run the same winding derivation, get the cell string. (Three of the thirteen — those with bow-tie faces — wait on the §3.4 fix first.)

4.2 D2 — custom plane sets: 55 → 126

The single largest rung, 71 figures, and the design choice is already half-present. The Java applet has a custom-planes dialog: enter [(nx,ny,nz)…] or [(nx,ny,nz,px,py,pz)…], pick any symmetry group, stellate the arrangement, save as .stel with the __PLANES source. The port's parser already reads that field; the preset layer throws it away and the app rejects any document whose polyhedron is not a catalog name. Wiring that one path through buildStellation — which takes an arbitrary {n,d} list plus matrices today — restores it.

What it unlocks splits in two. First, the famous compounds in “alien” planes: two cubes on a 3-fold axis (VIII-3), three cubes (IX-23, Escher's), three octahedra (VIII-12, Escher's), two dodecahedra (VIII-31), six sphenoids (VIII-10), six boxes (VIII-22), and the polar of the final stellation (XII-10/16). A verified subtlety on VIII-12: the three-octahedra compound is a stellation of a tetrakis hexahedron variety whose 24 planes are the signed permutations of (0,1,√2)/√3 — not the Catalan solid d13 (signed permutations of (0,1,2)/√5, ≈8.7° away; 0/24 planes match). The compound tier needs its own plane sets; no shipped kernel substitutes. Second, the long tail of Brückner's Part-F “combination” solids — cube+octahedron, two-tetrahedra, dodecahedron+icosahedron families in 8–62 planes — where he explores varieties of the same plane arrangement at different distance ratios. That is precisely a custom-planes workflow: same normals, slide the ratios. (It is also where the caveat lives: for several varieties Brückner states the ratio only qualitatively, so the kernels are one parameter short of automatic.)

Compounds are stellations — in exactly one sense

Verified as a theorem, not an analogy: any finite union of closed orientable solids equals a union of cells in the arrangement of its own face planes (each open cell is disjoint from every boundary, hence entirely in or out). That is why five cubes fell to {0,1,2,3,4} on d29, and why every compound on the plates is reachable once its plane set can be entered. The converse boundary is just as firm: a one-sided surface bounds nothing, so no cell union will ever be XII-5 (§4.5).

4.3 D3 — dihedral families: 126 → 139

Thirteen figures are prisms, antiprisms, crown polyhedra and stephanoids — Brückner's axial chapter (VIII-4, 13, 15–20, 24, 40, 41; X-17's four-tetrahedra compound; X-21). The remarkable fact from the parity audit: the port ships the entire dihedral symmetry apparatus already — symmetry.json carries C2…C12 and D2…D12 in h/d/v variants — and it is dead data, because no catalog solid has an axial point group and there is no custom-planes path. Kaleido's prism families (#1–5) were never shipped as files, in the applet either. The unlock is parametric: generate {p/q}-prism and antiprism plane sets (or geometry) on demand. One repair rides along: twenty of the 85 symmetry entries (C8–C12, C8h–C12h, C8v–C12v, D8h–D12h) ship with empty matrix arrays in both the applet and the port — selecting them silently yields no group. The stephanoids need exactly these Dn entries to be real.

4.4 D4 — diametral planes: 139 → 140

Covered in §3.3: one direct figure (VIII-1, the orientable octahemioctahedron), plus prerequisite status inside earlier rungs for IX-1, IX-18, XII-1, and honest arrangements for ten orphan files. Ranked here not because it is deep — the arrangement algebra never uses d>0 — but because its blast radius is semantics and UI (layer 0, presets, the Cells table) rather than one function.

4.5 D5 — the one-sided remainder: display, not cells

Five figures can never be cell unions, by the theorem above: VIII-29 (extended triakis octahedron), IX-10, X-16 (Möbius solids from truncated solids), XII-1, XII-5. The book is explicit that these are einseitig — Brückner's §143/§148/§163 one-sided chapter is where his subject leaves Coxeter's future cell-world entirely. Showing them needs a face-set renderer — draw the self-intersecting faces as given in geometry.json, no arrangement involved — which is a small, self-contained viewer mode, and incidentally the honest way to show any star polyhedron “as Brückner built it”: his paper models are surfaces, not solids.

5Symmetry as the second design axis

The session's other capability thread was symmetry. Two findings sharpen it. First, the port's subgroup menu is computed (by matrix containment over top-level groups), where the applet used a hand-written pairing table — and the computation loses all ten icosahedral-frame subgroups (D5d(I) D5(I) S10(I) C5v(I) C5(I) D3d(I) D3(I) S6(I) C3v(I) C3(I)): they exist only as axis-aligned variants inside the applet's table, not as top-level entries. Since 109 of the 121 catalog solids are icosahedral, the practical loss is every 5-fold and 3-fold partial-symmetry stellation — the “pillar” and chiral-axis selections. Second, the granularity story: the engine's two-level design (full group for cell types, one chiral subgroup for hand-splitting) is what made the five-tetrahedra compound selectable; each further subgroup exposed multiplies selectable subsets and multiplies UI rows in equal measure — the transcript's C3 complaint (a group list long enough that it no longer fits without scrolling) is the cost curve of the same choice that Miller's rules were invented to cap (§6).

6Brückner, Coxeter, and where these choices come from

The design space this report walks was mapped a century before the software. Three traditions correspond to three representation choices:

Brückner thinks in faces. His method is the Grenzfläche: read a boundary polygon off the complete figure (the stellation diagram), then build the paper surface. His §153 description of the icosahedron's complete figure handles the twentieth, parallel plane projectively — “Die Ebene 20 schneidet die Zeichenebene in der unendlichweiten Geraden” — the plane at infinity is just another line of the figure. The irony writes itself: the 1900 book is more projectively complete than the affine d > 0 shortcut in the 2000s software; and Brückner's one-sided §148 models with faces through the centre are exactly the cases the point encoding excludes. His face-first stance is also why one-sided solids belong in his book at all: a surface needs no interior.

Coxeter–Du Val think in cells. The Fifty-Nine Icosahedra (1938) replaced the boundary-polygon reading with the cell-set reading this engine implements — Du Val's a…h layer notation is literally the app's {0,…,7}. Miller's rules are then a selection-criteria design choice over the same arrangement: symmetry constraints (his rules fix the rotation group I, admitting the two chiral hands) plus connectivity filters to cut 2N subsets to a publishable 59. Every later fight — Pawley's fully-supported criterion, Inchbald's critique that cells can't distinguish the medial from the great triambic icosahedron — is an argument about that same lever, and the app's shift-click “add with support” is Pawley's criterion as a gesture.

The compounds tradition thinks in plane sets. Five cubes as a stellation of the triacontahedron (verified here as shells 0–4 of d29) goes back through Hess to Cauchy's 1812 memoir; Luke (1957) did the rhombic dodecahedron, Ede (1958) and later Messer the triacontahedron — each of these is a catalog choice: which plane arrangements are worth naming as a “solid to stellate.” The applet's custom-planes dialog was Bulatov's generalization of exactly that decision to the user, and D2 is its restoration.
